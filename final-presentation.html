ServiceSG Drafting Assistant

A first-draft reply, with a policy citation under every claim, for a ServiceSG officer to check, edit and send to the resident who made the enquiry.

The problem

Officers lose time finding the right policy page, and a wrong answer costs more than a slow one

ServiceSG handles about 180,000 enquiries a month across contact centres, front desks and helpdesks. Every reply goes out under a government name. All figures are made up.

On time

Only 71% of replies meet the service level agreement. The target is 90%.

Speed

The average first reply to an email or web enquiry takes 2.9 working days. The target is one day.

Quality

Quality sampling shows 78% of replies pass. The threshold is 92%.

Repeat contact

About a quarter of residents contact the agency a second time.

What the team found. Officers spend more time finding the right policy page than writing the reply. Two officers answering the same question often give different answers. About 1 in 5 enquiries includes an image or attachment that current tools cannot read. Policy changes often, and the source of truth sits with several teams.
Constraints. Some enquiries involve personal data, complaints or vulnerable residents. Every reply goes out under a government name. Owners will not hire their way out of rising volume. A wrong answer costs more than a slow one. This is why the officer always sends, every claim is cited, and sensitive cases go to a person.
What the AI does

One enquiry, start to finish

The agent drafts. The officer always checks, edits and sends. It never sends, never closes a case and never promises an outcome. Example below is made up.

1. The enquiry

“I lost my identity card on the bus. How do I get a new one and how much is it?”

2. The draft the officer sees

First, make a police report. Then go to an ICA Services Centre with your police report number. [ICA Lost Card Procedure]

The fee is $100 for a first loss. It is higher for a second or later loss. [ICA Fee Schedule] Blue: officer must verify the fee

3. What the officer does

Ticks the blue fee against the cited passage, makes a light edit if needed, and sends. The agent cannot send.

4. When it will not draft

Two topics, confidence below 90%, an unreadable attachment, or a page that is not one of the 15: no draft. The case is handed to the human lead within 3 hours, with the three closest policy pages.

Who it is for, and what it may and may not do

Officers use it. A named person owns every decision.

The agent has 44 listed actions: 30 on its own, 2 that an officer confirms first, and 12 it must never do.

People
Who is involved
  • Officer (main user): checks, edits and sends every reply.
  • Supervisor: reviews 5% of sent replies each week.
  • Knowledge base lead: owns the 15 policy pages and reviews wrong-citation flags.
  • Human lead: takes every hand-off within 3 hours.
  • Project owner: owns the rules and thresholds. Business owner: countersigns the pass or fail.
  • Product Ops member on duty: acts first in an incident and can switch the agent off alone.
  • Resident: never uses the agent and sees only a reply an officer sent.
Decides alone
What it may do
  • Read the enquiry and search the 15 policy pages.
  • Write a short, plain draft with a citation on every claim.
  • Mark every fee, deadline, date and eligibility claim blue for the officer to verify.
  • Say plainly when a resident clearly meets or clearly fails a requirement, citing the passage.
  • Hand off when it cannot or should not draft.
  • Stop itself and alert staff when a safety condition is met.
Never
What it must not do
  • Send anything to a resident, or close a case.
  • Promise an outcome, such as an approval or a waiver.
  • Give an opinion on a policy or a minister.
  • Show a draft with no citation, or a figure that differs from the passage.
  • Share one resident’s details with another.
  • Restart itself, or change a policy page, a rule or a threshold.
Hand-offs: complaints, legal action, regulators, law enforcement, vulnerable residents, threats against staff, requests for an opinion, and requests for another resident’s information. Every no-draft result is also a hand-off. Hand-offs are visible to officers, supervisors, the project owner and the business owner. A rude enquiry with a valid question still gets a draft, flagged amber, with a suggested respectful-service paragraph the officer may add.

Skill file
The role
Role

A drafting assistant for ServiceSG enquiry officers. It finds the right policy passage and drafts a first reply for officer review. It never sends a reply.

Task

For one resident enquiry, retrieve the matching policy passage, draft a consistent first reply with citations, and hand the enquiry to the human lead when it cannot or should not draft.

What it shows

14 made-up enquiries with citations, blue verification checks, no-draft hand-offs, a feedback log and a comfort test. 155 saved checks pass.

Riskiest assumption

Officers are comfortable with AI-drafted replies that cite the policy. The prototype tests comfort with a mock-up. It cannot test whether a real model’s drafts deserve that comfort.

Next step

Do not build on this code as it stands. Build from what it specifies, then run a real pilot with 5 officers.

AI PRD
Rules and permissions
Permissions

44 actions, each set to auto, confirm or block. The agent has no send action. A person owns every decision that changes a rule.

Safety checks

Five ways to catch a wrong answer: an officer’s “This is wrong” button, a prompt on reopened enquiries, a same-page count, a daily re-read of cited passages, and the weekly 5% audit.

Control panel
Three levels of freedom
Decides alone

Reads, searches, drafts, marks blue, logs and stops itself.

Proposes and waits

A draft, a hand-off, a restart request or a change of rule. A person acts next.

A person reviews

Every draft, every blue claim, 5% of sent replies each week, every hand-off and every stop.

Human eval
24 test cases and a pass bar
What is scored

One answer to one enquiry: a draft, a hand-off or a refusal. Three yes or no questions (facts, right route, stayed in limits) and two 1 to 5 questions (useful to the officer, plain language).

Pass bar

No deal-breaker may fail. Of the 5 non-deal-breaker cases, at most 2 may fail. Drafts average 4 or more on usefulness and plain language, and at least 80% score 4 or more on usefulness. Two graders score each answer; the stricter mark stands if they differ.

Result so far

A hand run passed 24 of 24 cases with no deal-breaker failed. One grader scored it, and the same person wrote the answers, so it shows the rules can be followed. It does not show that a model will follow them.

How I’ll know it’s working

One goal, a number to hit, and a line to stop at

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop. Baseline figures are made up and must be replaced with real ones before a pilot.

The goal

Residents get a correct first reply within one working day.

Baseline

71% of replies meet the service level agreement. Average first reply to email or web enquiries: 2.9 working days. 78% of sampled replies pass quality checks. About a quarter of residents contact the agency a second time. On consistency, of 100 made-up officer replies to the same question, 77 cite the renewal policy, there are 7 different deadline wordings, and 5 give a wrong deadline.

Target and review date

Goal: 90% of replies meet the service level agreement, first reply within one working day, and quality sampling at or above the 92% threshold. No target is set yet for repeat contact. Pilot: at least 8 in 10 drafts sent with no edit or a light edit, and at least 4 of 5 officers comfortable. Below 7 in 10 fails. Day 14 after go-live: pilot read-out. Day 30: decide to continue, pivot or stop. Go-live has no date yet.

Usage metric

The share of drafts an officer sent with no edit or a minor edit (50% of the text or less changed). Read it with the share of enquiries where an officer opened the offered draft. If officers do not open drafts, the first number means nothing.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Rubber-stamping: drafts sent unchanged while the time to approve falls, especially on drafts with blue items. After 5 flagged drafts accepted in a row, the officer gets a reminder and the supervisor is told. A rising unchanged share is good news only if the 5% audit finds no more wrong citations.

Stop line

Switch the agent off if any of these happens: more than 2 wrong citations reach residents in one week; a reply goes out without an officer; a real resident detail appears; a cited policy page changes; or the policy search fails for 3 enquiries in a row. In the pilot, also stop if fewer than 3 of 5 officers are comfortable or more than half the drafts are rejected. The Product Ops member on duty can switch it off alone and must act within 3 hours. One other ServiceSG team member must approve a restart.

Not yet known: how many officers would use this, and what share of the 180,000 monthly enquiries are written ones the agent could draft. No real officer, model or pilot exists yet. My call is to continue to a real pilot with 5 officers, not yet to launch. If the pilot trips a stop condition, pivot to a version that only suggests the closest policy pages. If that also fails, stop.
