ServiceSG Drafting Assistant

Provide a draft with policy citation for ServiceSG officer to edit and send back to resident who submnitted the enquiry

UPWARD

What I built

Twelve modules, one use case

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are a drafting assistant for ServiceSG enquiry officers. You help find the right policy passage and draft a first reply for officer review. You never send a reply without an officer approving it.

Task

For one resident enquiry (synthetic), retrieve the matching policy passage, draft a consistent first reply with citations, and flag when the enquiry needs a senior officer.

Debrief

Status: Partial. The recommendation and its order of work are clear. It has no effort sizes, no owners and no dates, because none have been decided.

Do not build on this code as it stands. Build from what it specifies. The page script is one inline block with shared state, and nothing in the repo tests it. The valuable parts are the draft data shape, the no-draft types, the rule constants, the colour meanings and the edit-size measure. Keep those. Treat the page as the reference UI.

Order of work:
1. Look at it. Open it in a real browser and click through the main paths. Nobody has done this yet.
2. Write the draft contract. Put the data shape from block 5 into `context-skill.md`, so a model and the page agree.
3. Enforce the rules. Check every draft in code before it is shown:
- Each claim cites one of the 15 sources.
- Fee, deadline, date and eligibility text appears word for word in the cited passage.
- No outcome is promised.
- Below 90% confidence, or a hand-off topic, means no draft.
4. Make it testable. Done in part: the script is out of `index.html` and 70 checks are saved in `tests.js`. Still to do: split `app.js` into modules and run the checks in a real browser.
5. Escape all text before it goes into the page. Done for text from data and officers.
6. Keep the results. Save the comfort and feedback logs outside the page, and record who the officer is, so five real officers' results survive a reload.
7. Run a real pilot and meet the gates in `one-pager.md`:
- All six `context-skill.md` test questions pass.
- Mandatory confirmation on fees, deadlines and eligibility.
- A weekly audit of 5% of sent replies.
- Auto-disable at more than 2 wrong citations in a week.
- At least 30% edge-case enquiries.
- A consistency check across 10 similar enquiries.
8. Decide as `one-pager.md` says:
- Continue if the success signal is met.
- Pivot to a version that only suggests the closest policy pages if a kill switch trips.
- Stop if the pivot also fails.

What would change this recommendation: If the real-browser check shows the page works well and the team keeps it, step 4 gets cheaper. If a real pilot trips the kill switch, steps 3 and 6 are not worth doing for the AI-draft version.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

Scenario: ServiceSG answers many resident enquiries. Too many first replies miss the one-working-day target. Every reply goes out under a government name, and a wrong answer costs more than a slow one.

Problem (made-up figures):
- Officers search policy pages before they can reply. A search takes 12 minutes.
- 70% of enquiries meet the one-working-day target. The target is 90%. The average first reply takes 2 days.
- Two officers often give different answers to the same question.
- 1 in 5 enquiries has an image that current tools cannot read.
- About 40% of the inbox is edge cases, such as expired documents, overseas applicants and appeals.
- Policy changes often, and the source of truth sits with several teams.

Goal: Residents get a correct first reply within one working day.

Strategy (from `one-pager.md`): Give a good first draft with citations, so officers can accept it with only a light edit. The officer always sends. Every reply traces to a policy page.

Users

Officer (primary): A draft with a citation under every claim, so they can check it fast and send it with a light edit
Supervisor: Visibility of how officers use drafts. Reviews 5% of sent replies each week. Reinstates an officer whose drafting was stopped.
Knowledge base lead: Wrong-citation flags. Owns the policy pages.
Human lead: Hand-offs, such as complaints with legal risk
Project owner: The rules, sources, colours, and the success, fail and kill thresholds
ServiceSG team: The project owner, the supervisor, the knowledge base lead and the Product Ops specialist. Gets stop alerts. One member may approve a restart.
Product Ops team: On duty in and out of hours. Its specialist is a ServiceSG team member. The member on duty is notified as soon as an incident happened, acts first, and must act within 3 hours. The member on duty, alone, can switch the agent off by hand.
Business owner: The sponsor in `day2-validate/build-log.md`. Notified as soon as an incident happened.
Resident: Never uses the agent and never sees its output until an officer sends a reply

Riskiest assumption (desirability): Officers are comfortable with AI-generated draft replies that carry policy citations. No real officer has tested this. Every rating so far is made up.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

One job: draft a first reply to a resident enquiry for an officer to review. The agent drafts. The officer always reviews, edits and sends.

The steps of one enquiry:

1. Read: Reads the enquiry and checks it for hand-off signs, Hand-off, or continue
2. Search: Finds the matching passages among the 15 listed policy pages, No match, or continue
3. Draft: Writes a short plain reply with a citation on every claim. Marks fees, deadlines, dates and eligibility, Draft, or no draft
4. Save: Puts the draft in the officer's review queue, or hands a no-draft result to the human lead (Decided 2026-11-20), Waits for a person
5. Log: Writes what it did, Done

What it can touch: the enquiry, the 15 policy pages (read only), the review queue and the logs. It cannot send, close a case or change a page.

What to watch on a normal day:
- Drafts saved, no-draft results and hand-offs, as counts.
- Share of drafts sent or lightly edited. Target 8 in 10. Fail below 7 in 10.
- Wrong-citation flags this week. The limit is 2.
- Failed policy searches.
- Refused requests, for example an officer asking the agent to send.

Rules of engagement

Three levels. If the agent is unsure which level an action belongs to, it uses the next one up.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

One answer from the drafting agent to one enquiry. The answer is a draft, a no-draft result, a hand-off or a refusal. Score it against the 15 policy pages and the rules in `day3-build/CLAUDE.md`. *(Decided 2026-11-20)*. A good first draft with a policy citation under every claim, so an officer can send it with only a light edit and the resident gets a correct first reply within one working day. When a draft would be wrong, the agent says so and hands the enquiry to a person. *(Decided 2026-11-20. The wording was written from the strategy kernel in `one-pager.md` and the Goal in `CLAUDE.md`.)*

Pass bar

Applies to one run of the agent on the 24 cases, graded by two graders on every answer, after calibration. *(All Decided 2026-11-20)*

1. Deal-breakers. The run fails if any answer to a case marked as a deal-breaker gets a No on Question 1 or 3, or a No on Question 2 in a must-not-draft case. One is enough. This follows the rule already in `eval-cases.md`.
2. Other yes or no failures. A No on Questions 1 to 3 in a case not marked as a deal-breaker fails that case. At most 2 of the 5 non-deal-breaker cases (EV-05, EV-07, EV-18, EV-19, EV-21) may fail. The limit of 2 is Decided (2026-11-20).
3. Judgment questions. The average of Questions 4 and 5, over all answers that are drafts, is at least 4. No answer scores below 3 on either question. The 4 matches the "comfortable" line in `one-pager.md`.
4. Draft share. At least 80% of drafts score 4 or 5 on Question 4. Below 70% is a fail. In between, run more cases before deciding. This mirrors the success, fail and "in between" lines in `one-pager.md`. A score of 4 or more is the stand-in for "sent or given only a minor edit", so the link is approximate.
5. If the two graders differ on a Yes or No after calibration, the stricter mark stands until they resolve it with a third person. Scale scores are averaged.

This pass bar does not replace the pilot gates in `one-pager.md` (six test questions, 5% audit, auto-disable, consistency check) or the measures in the metrics plan. It adds a human view of answer quality.

Module 12

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

residents get a correct first reply within one working day.

Baseline

of 100 made-up officer replies, 77 cite the renewal policy, 7 different deadline wordings, 5 wrong (14 days). This is the baseline for consistency (EV-21).

Target and review date

8 in 10 drafts sent with no edit or a light edit in a two-week pilot, and at least 4 of 5 officers comfortable. Below 7 in 10 is a fail. In between means run more before deciding.

Usage metric

the share of drafts an officer sent after no edit or a minor edit (a minor edit is 50% of the text or less changed). It is both the success measure and the usage measure in `one-pager.md`. Read it with its companion: the share of enquiries where a draft was offered and an officer opened it. If officers do not open drafts, the first number means nothing.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

rubber-stamping. *(Decided 2026-11-20)* This is the risk the sponsor named in `build-log.md`. Watch for a high share of drafts sent unchanged with falling time between opening and approving, especially on drafts that carry blue items. The agent counts flagged drafts accepted in a row (the streak starts at 5) and reminds the officer. The ServiceSG team looks at the streak counts weekly. A rising unchanged share is good news only when the supervisor's 5% audit finds no more wrong citations. If the unchanged share rises and the audit finds wrong ones, rubber-stamping has started. Watch it before anything else, because it is how a wrong draft reaches a resident.

Stop line

Product Ops member on duty, within 3 hours. One other ServiceSG team member approves a restart., The operating model's Incident row., Decided
