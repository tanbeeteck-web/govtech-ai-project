# Handoff note

**Scenario:** A · ServiceSG enquiry response
**Prototype:** `day2-validate/prototype/`
**Written:** 2026-11-18

Everything in this note is made up. No real resident, officer or record is used. For full detail see `living-prd.md`. If this note and the code disagree, the code is right.

## What it does

A clickable mock-up of an enquiry inbox for ServiceSG officers. It tests one assumption: **officers are comfortable with draft replies with policy citations generated by AI.**

- An officer opens one of 10 enquiries. Five have a draft with a policy citation under each claim. Five have no draft, and the screen says why.
- Fees, deadlines and eligibility show in blue. The officer must tick a checklist before approving.
- The officer can approve, edit, reject or flag a citation. Then they rate their comfort from 1 to 5.
- A panel turns the ratings into a verdict: success, fail, kill switch or in between.
- A test checkbox simulates a failed policy lookup. No draft is shown, the officer's typed text is kept, and the officer can retry.
- Nothing is ever sent.

To open it, double-click `prototype/index.html`. To run the 70 saved checks, follow the steps in `prototype/README.md`.

## What is synthetic

Everything. In particular:

- **There is no AI.** Every draft, citation, confidence figure and topic is written by hand in `data.js`.
- **The policy lookup is fake.** It fails only when the test checkbox is on and the enquiry is about fees.
- **The 15 policy pages, their fees, dates and passages** are invented. All dates are in 2024.
- **The officers O1 to O5** are a dropdown. One tester types every rating.
- **The numbers in `opportunity.md`** (12-minute search, 2-day reply, 70% on time against a 90% target, 1 in 5 with an image) are made up. The 70% is a new made-up figure, not a measurement.
- **The thresholds** were chosen by the learner. They are not from a pilot.
- **Buttons that only show a message:** Start from scratch, Open case file, Escalate, Hand off. "View full policy" links go nowhere.

## What is missing for production

**Not built, and required by earlier documents:**
- The 30-second signal that officers are stuck. Seconds to decide is recorded and never used.
- Auto-disable when more than 2 wrong citations reach residents in a week. A flag is logged, but nothing counts them.
- The weekly audit of 5% of sent replies.
- The check that 10 similar enquiries get the same wording.
- A test set where at least 30% of enquiries are edge cases. The data has 5 of 10 by hand, and nothing measures it.

**Not built, and needed to be real:**
- Any AI model, and any real policy search.
- Code that enforces the rules in `context-skill.md`. Nothing checks that a claim cites one of the 15 sources, that a fee matches its passage word for word, or that no outcome is promised. The 90% rule only holds because the hand-set data fits it.
- Saved results. A reload clears the ratings, the log and typed text.
- Real officers, sign-in and roles (supervisor, human lead, knowledge base lead).
- Reading attachments. The one attachment case is always unreadable.
- A check that policy pages are current.
- A way to send a reply, by design. The officer sends.

**Not checked:**
- Nobody has opened the page in a real browser. Whether the buttons fit a laptop screen is an estimate.
- The 70 saved checks run on a stand-in page. They test the logic and the escaped text. They do not test how the page looks or whether a click works.

## Decisions an engineer must not change without asking

Ask the learner first. Each one protects trust or sets a test result.

1. **The officer always sends.** No code path may send a reply or close a case. The AI drafts only.
2. **Colour meanings** (see `context-skill.md`):
   - Blue means the officer must verify. It does not mean low confidence.
   - Green means nothing to verify.
   - Red means stop: no draft, or the comfort test failed.
   - Never red on a section of a draft. Never green while any section is blue.
   - Every colour also has words.
3. **Fees, deadlines, dates and eligibility** must match the cited passage word for word. Each one is marked and the officer must confirm it before approving.
4. **Every claim cites a policy page by name.** A draft with no citation is never shown. This includes the failed-lookup case.
5. **No draft** below 90% confidence, for hand-off cases (complaint, legal action, regulator, vulnerable resident), when the answer is not in a source, or when an attachment cannot be read. The tool lists the closest three policy pages instead.
6. **Never promise an outcome.** No draft says an appeal, waiver or application will be approved.
7. **Text the officer typed is never lost,** except when the officer presses "Discard my edit".
8. **The test thresholds** in `log.js`:
   - Success: at least 4 of 5 officers comfortable (average 4 or more) and at least 80% of drafts used.
   - Fail: below 70% used.
   - Kill switch: 3 of 5 officers reject, or majorly edit, more than half of their drafts. It also trips if fewer than 3 officers are comfortable, or if more than 50% of drafts are rejected.
   - Major edit: more than 50% of the words changed. The tool measures it. The tester does not choose.
9. **Made-up data only.** No real names, NRIC numbers or personal details.
10. **Text from data and officers is escaped** before it goes into the page. Keep the check in `tests.js`.
11. **The Head of Product objection** in `build-log.md` still quotes "90% already meet SLA". The learner chose to leave it. Do not correct it without asking.

## Handoff decision

**Decision (the learner's): move to a production spec now.**

The learner did not record their own reasons. The reasons below come from what the prototype settled, and are marked as such.

**What the prototype has settled, so a spec can be written:**
- The screens and flows are mapped from the code (`living-prd.md`, block 3).
- The rules, colour meanings and no-draft cases are written down (`context-skill.md`).
- The draft data shape is defined, so a model and the page can agree on it.
- The test thresholds are named and in the code.
- The unsafe text handling is fixed and 70 checks are saved.

**Risks the learner is accepting by moving now.** None of these is resolved. The spec must not treat them as settled:
1. **The riskiest assumption is untested.** No real officer has used it. Every rating so far is made up. If real officers are not comfortable with AI drafts, the draft tool may not be worth building.
2. **Feasibility is unknown.** No model has run the six `context-skill.md` test questions.
3. **Four safeguards are not built.** The 30-second signal, auto-disable, the weekly audit and the consistency check have to be designed in the spec, not assumed.
4. **The thresholds came from one person.** Five officers is a small sample.
5. **The layout was never seen in a real browser.**

**How the spec must manage those risks (conditions, not options):**
- The spec's first milestone is a real run with 5 officers, using made-up enquiries. It uses the success signal in `build-log.md`: at least 4 of 5 comfortable and at least 80% of drafts used.
- Build work on the AI draft path starts only after that milestone passes.
- If a kill switch trips, stop the draft path and pivot to the version that only suggests the closest policy pages, as `one-pager.md` says.
- All six `context-skill.md` test questions must pass on a real model before any real officer sees a draft.
- The four missing safeguards are in the spec as launch gates.
- The spec keeps every item in "Decisions an engineer must not change without asking".

**Next steps, in order:**
1. Write the production spec from `living-prd.md`. Start with block 8, the engineering recommendation.
2. Open the page in a real browser and fix what looks wrong.
3. Run the six `context-skill.md` test questions on a real model.
4. Run the comfort test with 5 real officers on made-up enquiries.
5. Review the thresholds after that run.
